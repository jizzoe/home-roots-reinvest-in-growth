# M1.1 Speech Corpus — Recording Guide and Utterance Script

Date: 2026-09-06
Purpose: Collect the synthetic audio corpus that M1.1 Offline Multilingual Speech is measured against.
Companion: [V1 Scope Map and Milestone Plan](../design-briefs/V1%20Scope%20Map%20and%20Milestone%20Plan.md), M1.1 section

## Who records what

| Language | Speaker | Device |
| --- | --- | --- |
| English | Joe Rice | iPhone |
| French | JLP | Android |
| Haitian Creole | JLP, or another Haitian Creole speaker | Android |

Each speaker records the same 30 utterances, one file per utterance. Three
languages gives 90 files. If a second speaker per language is available, a second
pass is valuable but not required for the first benchmark.

## The rule that makes this corpus worth collecting

**Do not generate the audio with a text-to-speech tool, and do not re-record an
utterance until it sounds clean.** Synthesized or over-rehearsed speech is evenly
paced, accent-free, and noise-free. A recognizer scores far better on it than on
a real person in a real market, and the benchmark would tell us nothing about the
product. Natural first takes, with hesitations and background sound, are the
point.

Keep a take even if the speaker stumbles, corrects themselves, or trails off.
Those are the cases the product must survive.

## Recording applications

### Android — recommended: ASR Voice Recorder (NLL Apps)

- Free, one tap per recording, each take saved as its own file.
- Set the format to **WAV / PCM** in settings if available; otherwise the highest
  quality option offered.
- Alternative if that app is unavailable: **Easy Voice Recorder** (Digipom).
  Simpler interface, but confirm it is not compressing to a low bitrate.

### iOS — recommended: Voice Memos (built in)

- Already installed, one recording per take, straightforward export.
- Set **Settings → Voice Memos → Audio Quality → Lossless** before starting.
- Alternative if a true WAV file is wanted: **Awesome Voice Recorder**.

### Settings that matter, on either platform

- Mono is fine. 16 kHz or higher sample rate. Uncompressed if the option exists.
- Hold the phone the way you would normally hold it while speaking to it, not
  against your mouth and not across the room.
- Leave about one second of silence before and after each utterance.

## Two passes, two environments

Record the full set twice:

1. **Quiet pass** — a room with no background noise.
2. **Ambient pass** — outdoors, in a market, or with normal household or street
   sound in the background. Do not add noise artificially.

If time only allows one and a half passes, do the full quiet pass and at least
ten ambient utterances.

## File naming

`<lang>-<id>-<pass>.wav`, for example `ht-04-quiet.wav`, `fr-17-ambient.wav`.
Language codes: `en`, `fr`, `ht`. Pass: `quiet`, `ambient`.

## Sending the files

Use Google Drive, a file-transfer link, or email attachments.

**Do not send the recordings through WhatsApp.** WhatsApp re-encodes audio to a
low-bitrate format, which changes exactly the signal the benchmark measures.

## How the script works

The table gives the **intent** and an English and French phrasing. Speakers should
say it **the way they would naturally say it**, not read the line word for word.
Natural phrasing is more valuable than fidelity to the script.

The Haitian Creole column is deliberately empty. Machine-translated Creole read
aloud would produce unnatural speech and would bias the benchmark. The Creole
speaker should hear or read the intent and say it in their own words.

**Ground truth is what was actually said, not what the script says.** After
recording, the speaker or a fluent listener writes down the exact words spoken
for each file. That transcript, plus the amount actually stated, becomes the
label. A take where the speaker said a different number than the script is still
a perfectly good take, as long as the label matches the audio.

## What comes back

For each language, deliver:

1. The audio files, named as above.
2. A transcript file listing, per file, the exact words spoken and the amount
   actually stated (or "none").
3. Any note about the recording conditions worth knowing — where it was recorded,
   what the background sound was, anything that went wrong.

The corpus is then frozen and split into development and sealed-holdout sets
before any engine work begins. As with the receipt corpus, a benchmark assembled
after the implementation is a benchmark shaped by the implementation.
