# M1.1 Offline Multilingual Speech Design Brief

## 1. Problem and desired outcome
Problem: M1 proves the speech proposal and confirmation interaction with deterministic transcripts and device TTS, but it does not prove real offline multilingual speech on the inexpensive Android devices and iPhones the product must support.
Desired outcome: M1.1 produces physical-device evidence for offline STT and TTS in English, French, and Haitian Creole, while preserving manual fallback, editable proposals, explicit confirmation, low recurring service cost, and replaceable speech engines.

## 3. Options considered and tradeoffs
- Whisper-first offline spike behind SpeechToTextAdapter — begin with multilingual Whisper `tiny`, which covers the three required STT languages with no per-minute provider fee, but still has app-size, native-integration, latency, memory, CPU, and battery risk on low-cost phones.
- Vosk-first offline spike — lighter and mature on constrained Android devices for English and French, but the available model catalog does not provide a maintained Haitian Creole model and its documented iPhone build route is less self-service; it cannot prove the complete cross-platform language requirement.
- Platform speech services — low direct cost and small application footprint, but offline recognition and Haitian Creole voice/model availability vary by operating system, installed engine, and device.
- Cloud speech services — may improve managed language options, but do not satisfy the offline milestone and introduce recurring cost, connectivity, privacy, retention, and governance work.

## 5. Scope, non-goals, constraints, dependencies, and risks
- Scope: A mobile-repository spike for short transaction utterances and confirmation read-back on representative low-cost Android hardware and an approved iPhone; offline English, French, and Haitian Creole evaluation; microphone permission and unavailable-engine states; a replaceable SpeechToTextAdapter with an initial multilingual Whisper `tiny` implementation; a replaceable TTS boundary; synthetic benchmark fixtures; and evidence for accuracy, amount/currency capture, latency, memory, app size, CPU/battery behavior, and manual fallback. Vosk may be evaluated as an Android-first English/French size, latency, and battery comparator, not as the Haitian Creole or cross-platform baseline.
- Non-goals: Selecting a permanent production provider before evidence; cloud speech or backend synchronization; production localization certification; real participant audio; automatic creation of financial records; continuous dictation; receipt/OCR work; model training; app-store deployment; or changing the accepted M1 interaction contract.
- Constraints: Offline operation is mandatory for the tested speech path.; English, French, and Haitian Creole must each be evaluated.; No metered speech service is required for the baseline path.; Speech output remains a proposal and cannot write a financial record without user review and confirmation.; Manual entry and visible text remain available when STT or TTS is missing, slow, inaccurate, or denied.; Model assets, licenses, redistribution terms, and native build requirements must be documented.; This planning repository does not authorize component implementation, deployment, vendor accounts, paid services, or real data.
- Dependencies: M1 completion and verification.; The mobile component repository and its component-local OpenSpec proposal.; Representative physical Android and iPhone test devices.; A synthetic multilingual audio corpus covering HTG amounts, dates, local product terms, accents, and background noise.; An Expo prebuild/development-build path capable of native Whisper integration and bundled model assets.
- Risks: Whisper may be too slow, memory-heavy, battery-intensive, or large for the Android floor device.; Haitian Creole recognition may be materially worse than language-table inclusion suggests, especially for amounts and local terms.; Installed offline Haitian Creole TTS may be absent on target devices.; Native Whisper bindings may raise Expo prebuild, NDK/CMake, maintenance, or store-packaging risk.; Vosk lacks an official Haitian Creole model and its iPhone packaging/binding route must be verified before it is budgeted as a cross-platform comparator.; A benchmark that is too small or too clean could conceal field failures.; Engine-specific details could leak past adapters and make replacement expensive.

## 6. Open questions and blocking decisions
- Which Whisper runtime, quantization, and packaging strategy should be paired with the initial multilingual `tiny` model, and what evidence would justify escalating to `base`?
- What measurable pass/fail thresholds apply to transcription accuracy, HTG amount capture, latency, memory, app-size growth, and battery use on each representative device?
- Which iPhone model and OS version define the initial iOS floor?
- Which offline Haitian Creole TTS voice is available on the target devices, and what accessible fallback is required when none exists?
- What synthetic speaker, accent, vocabulary, and noise matrix is sufficient for a credible three-language benchmark?
- Should TextToSpeechAdapter be mandated alongside the owner-confirmed SpeechToTextAdapter, or remain a recommended replaceable boundary?
