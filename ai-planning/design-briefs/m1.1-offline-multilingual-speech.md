# M1.1 Offline Multilingual Speech Design Brief

## 1. Problem and desired outcome
Problem: M1 proves the speech proposal and confirmation interaction with deterministic transcripts and device TTS, but it does not prove real offline multilingual speech on the inexpensive Android devices and iPhones the product must support.
Desired outcome: M1.1 produces physical-device evidence for offline STT and TTS in English, French, and Haitian Creole, while preserving manual fallback, editable proposals, explicit confirmation, low recurring service cost, and replaceable speech engines.

## 2. Evidence and key findings

- [Whisper and Vosk local mobile STT research](../research/mobile-text-to-speech/whisper-vosk-local-stt/whisper-vosk-local-stt-findings.md): establishes multilingual Whisper `tiny` as the initial three-language benchmark configuration, Vosk as an English/French constrained-device comparator, and the current Vosk iPhone integration uncertainty.
- [V1 scope map](V1%20Scope%20Map%20and%20Milestone%20Plan.md): establishes M1, M1.1, and M1.2 ordering and the product-level acceptance direction.
- [M1 rapid thin-slice brief](m1-rapid-thin-slice-prototype.md): establishes the existing mocked transcript, device-TTS, manual fallback, and confirmation behavior.
- [Product guardrails](../../openspec/specs/v1-product-guardrails/spec.md): require offline-first access, Haitian Creole planning priority, accessible fallback, confirmation, and synthetic-only work.
- [Core domain model](../../openspec/specs/core-domain-model/spec.md): keeps raw input, proposal, edited proposal, and confirmed financial record separate.
- [M1 speech proposal design](../../openspec/changes/prototype-speech-proposal-confirmation/design.md): confirms that the current M1 speech slice is mocked and non-authoritative.

## 3. Options considered and tradeoffs
- Whisper-first offline spike behind SpeechToTextAdapter — begin with multilingual Whisper `tiny`, which covers the three required STT languages with no per-minute provider fee, but still has app-size, native-integration, latency, memory, CPU, and battery risk on low-cost phones.
- Vosk-first offline spike — lighter and mature on constrained Android devices for English and French, but the available model catalog does not provide a maintained Haitian Creole model and its documented iPhone build route is less self-service; it cannot prove the complete cross-platform language requirement.
- Platform speech services — low direct cost and small application footprint, but offline recognition and Haitian Creole voice/model availability vary by operating system, installed engine, and device.
- Cloud speech services — may improve managed language options, but do not satisfy the offline milestone and introduce recurring cost, connectivity, privacy, retention, and governance work.

## 5. Scope, non-goals, constraints, dependencies, and risks
- Scope: A mobile-repository spike for short transaction utterances and confirmation read-back on representative low-cost Android hardware and an approved iPhone; offline English, French, and Haitian Creole evaluation; microphone permission and unavailable-engine states; a replaceable SpeechToTextAdapter with an initial multilingual Whisper `tiny` implementation; a replaceable TTS boundary; synthetic benchmark fixtures; and evidence for accuracy, amount/currency capture, latency, memory, app size, CPU/battery behavior, and manual fallback. Vosk may be evaluated as an Android-first English/French size, latency, and battery comparator, not as the Haitian Creole or cross-platform baseline.
- Non-goals: Selecting a permanent production provider before evidence; cloud speech or backend synchronization; production localization certification; real participant audio; automatic creation of financial records; continuous dictation; receipt/OCR work; model training; app-store deployment; or changing the accepted M1 interaction contract.
- Constraints: Offline operation is mandatory for the tested speech path.; English, French, and Haitian Creole must each be evaluated.; No metered speech service is required for the baseline path.; Speech output remains a proposal and cannot write a financial record without user review and confirmation.; Manual entry and visible text remain available when STT or TTS is missing, slow, inaccurate, or denied.; Model assets, licenses, redistribution terms, and native build requirements must be documented.; This planning repository does not authorize component implementation, deployment, vendor accounts, paid services, or real data.
- Dependencies: M1 completion and verification.; The mobile component repository and its component-local OpenSpec proposal.; Representative physical Android and iPhone test devices.; A synthetic multilingual audio corpus covering HTG amounts, dates, local product terms, accents, and background noise.; An Expo prebuild/development-build path capable of native Whisper integration and bundled model assets.
- Risks: Whisper may be too slow, memory-heavy, battery-intensive, or large for the Android floor device.; Haitian Creole recognition may be materially worse than language-table inclusion suggests, especially for amounts and local terms.; Installed offline Haitian Creole TTS may be absent on target devices.; Native Whisper bindings may raise Expo prebuild, NDK/CMake, maintenance, or store-packaging risk.; Vosk lacks an official Haitian Creole model and its iPhone packaging/binding route must be verified before it is budgeted as a cross-platform comparator.; A benchmark that is too small or too clean could conceal field failures.; Engine-specific details could leak past adapters and make replacement expensive.

## 6. Open questions and blocking decisions
- Which Whisper runtime, quantization, and packaging strategy should be paired with the initial multilingual `tiny` model, and what evidence would justify escalating to `base`?
- What measurable pass/fail thresholds apply to transcription accuracy, HTG amount capture, latency, memory, app-size growth, and battery use on each representative device?
- Which iPhone model and OS version define the initial iOS floor?
- Which offline Haitian Creole TTS voice is available on the target devices, and what accessible fallback is required when none exists?
- What synthetic speaker, accent, vocabulary, and noise matrix is sufficient for a credible three-language benchmark?
- Should TextToSpeechAdapter be mandated alongside the owner-confirmed SpeechToTextAdapter, or remain a recommended replaceable boundary?

## 6a. Recommended answers to the open questions

Recorded 2026-09-06. These are recommendations from analysis of the existing
research, **not owner decisions**. They must be confirmed or amended before
OpenSpec Propose.

**Runtime, quantization, and packaging.** Use `whisper.rn` pinned to an exact
inspected version; it is the documented Expo-prebuild route with established
Android and iOS support. Use the multilingual `tiny` model, never `tiny.en`.
Measure unquantized `tiny` as the accuracy reference and one quantized variant
for footprint, reporting both. **Benchmark `base` in the same run as `tiny`**
rather than after it, so an escalation decision uses data already collected
instead of triggering a second cycle.

**Pass/fail thresholds.** Do not gate on word error rate; a badly wrong
transcript can still yield a correct proposal, and a good one can still lose the
amount. Gate on critical-field capture: was the HTG amount correct, and was the
transaction type correct. Apply the same precision-over-recall rule the receipt
work uses — an uncertain amount arrives blank rather than guessed.

Treat the per-language numbers as a routing decision rather than a uniform
pass/fail. English is the engineering gate that decides whether Whisper `tiny` is
viable at all. French and Haitian Creole are measured and reported, and their
numbers decide a product question: which languages get a speech entry point in
V1, and which fall back to manual entry with the visible review path. A Haitian
Creole threshold set today would be invented, because no evidence exists for
Whisper's Creole quality on bookkeeping speech.

Device thresholds on the representative U656AC, not an emulator: end of utterance
to proposal displayed within 5 seconds for a 10-second utterance; no
out-of-memory or crash across a 20-utterance session; no thermal throttling in
that session; app-size growth recorded and explicitly decided.

**iOS floor.** Recommend making iPhone a secondary portability check that runs
only after the Android result passes, rather than a co-equal gate. M1 deferred
iOS, the product guardrail is Android-priority, and a second device doubles the
native build surface and benchmark runs. If an iPhone number is wanted, name the
oldest available device and state plainly whether it establishes a floor or only
portability.

**Haitian Creole text-to-speech.** Do not attempt to source a Creole voice.
Record availability on the target devices as evidence, expect absence, and let
the already-accepted fallback stand: `SPEECH_LANGUAGE` maps `ht` to `fr-HT` and
the no-speech visible review path remains. One open question a Creole speaker can
settle quickly: whether a French voice reading Creole text is acceptable, or
whether Creole should default to the visible path with no read-back.

**Benchmark matrix.** Frozen corpus with a development and sealed-holdout split,
the same discipline as the receipt corpus. At least 30 utterances per language
across three languages. Dimensions: sale, expense and amount-only phrasings,
local product terms, code-switching, two to three speakers per language, and
quiet plus ambient noise passes. **The audio must be real recorded speech, never
text-to-speech output** — synthesized audio is clean, evenly paced and
accent-free, and a recognizer scores far better on it than on a person in a
market. That is the same failure as the vector-rendered receipt corpus, in a
different medium. The script and recording instructions are at
[M1.1 Speech Corpus Recording Guide](../plans/m1.1-speech-corpus-recording-guide.md).

**`TextToSpeechAdapter`.** Mandate it. The research states that speech-recognition
engine selection must not dictate the text-to-speech engine, and the boundary
already effectively exists in the delivered M1 `src/speech/tts.ts` work, so
naming it formally costs nothing.

**A seventh question the brief does not ask: model delivery.** A bundled `tiny`
model adds roughly 75 MiB to an APK currently measured in single-digit megabytes.
On low-cost phones with limited storage, and for testers installing over metered
data, that is a real product cost. The alternatives are to bundle it, matching
offline-first strictly, or to download it once during first-run setup over
WiFi with explicit user-facing states for the not-yet-downloaded case. This is a
product decision and should be answered before Propose, not discovered during it.
