# Enterprise Growth App 2, UXUI Product Guidelines v1.0

**Document 2**

**Enterprise Growth App**

**UX/UI Product Guidelines v1.0**

**Purpose**

The Enterprise Growth App serves entrepreneurs who may have limited bookkeeping experience.

The user experience must prioritize simplicity, trust, accessibility, and usefulness.

The application should feel like a business assistant—not an accounting system.

## 1. Core UX Principle

**Make the Entrepreneur Feel Capable**

The application should reinforce:

"I understand my business."

Not:

"I do not know accounting."

## 2. Language Principles

Avoid technical accounting terminology.

Avoid:

- Debit.
- Credit.
- Ledger.
- Assets.
- Liabilities.
- Balance sheet.

Use:

- Money received.
- Money spent.
- Sales.
- Expenses.
- Profit.
- What you earned.

## 3. Input Design Principles

Entrepreneurs should have multiple ways to enter information.

Preferred order:

## 1. Voice

Because speaking is often easier than typing.

## 2. Buttons

Simple selections.

Example:

"What happened?"

- I made a sale.
- I bought inventory.
- I paid an expense.

## 3. Camera

Receipt capture.

## 4. Typing

Only when necessary.

## 4. Confirmation Design

The system should clearly separate:

**What the entrepreneur entered**

from:

**What the application understood**

Example:

"You said:

Bought rice inventory for 5,000 HTG.

Is this correct?"

Buttons:

✓ Yes

✎ Edit

## 5. Dashboard Principles

The dashboard should answer:

**How is my business doing?**

Not:

**Here are financial statistics.**

Prioritize:

- Sales this week.
- Expenses this week.
- Profit.
- Cash available.
- What changed.
- What should I consider?

## 6. AI Interaction Principles

AI should behave like a helpful assistant.

Good:

"Your sales increased compared with last week."

Better:

"Your sales increased because you recorded more customer purchases."

Avoid:

"Your business is failing."

unless supported by reliable evidence.

## 7. Accessibility Principles

Design for:

- Older Android devices.
- Small screens.
- Bright outdoor environments.
- Limited connectivity.
- Users with different literacy levels.

## 8. Error Handling Principles

Errors should:

- Explain what happened.
- Protect user information.
- Provide next steps.

Avoid:

"Synchronization error 403."

Prefer:

"We saved your information. We will try again when the connection returns."

## 9. Trust Principles

The application should always communicate:

- What happened.
- What is saved.
- What requires confirmation.
- What is estimated.

Trust is more important than automation.
