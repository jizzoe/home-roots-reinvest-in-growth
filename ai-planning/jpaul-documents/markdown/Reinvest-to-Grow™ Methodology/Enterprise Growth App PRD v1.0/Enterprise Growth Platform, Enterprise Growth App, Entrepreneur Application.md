## 1. Product Overview

The Enterprise Growth App is the entrepreneur-facing component of the Home Roots Foundation (HRF) Enterprise Growth Platform.

The platform is designed to support HRF's **Reinvest-to-Grow™ Methodology**, which helps women entrepreneurs strengthen their businesses by improving business management, increasing access to affordable inputs, supporting responsible growth financing, and helping entrepreneurs retain and reinvest more income into their enterprises.

Version 1 of the Enterprise Growth App focuses on the **Business Journal Module**.

The Business Journal provides entrepreneurs with a simple, mobile-first way to:

- Record sales.
- Track expenses.
- Understand profit.
- Monitor cash movement.
- Organize business information.
- Build reliable business records.

The application is intentionally designed for entrepreneurs who may have:

- Limited bookkeeping experience.
- Limited access to traditional financial services.
- Limited digital experience.
- Intermittent internet connectivity.

The goal is not to create a traditional accounting system.

The goal is to create a simple business management tool that helps entrepreneurs understand and improve their businesses.

## 2. Strategic Context

Many entrepreneurs in emerging markets operate viable businesses but lack:

- Reliable business records.
- Visibility into profitability.
- Historical business data.
- Access to appropriate financing.
- Tools for making informed decisions.

Without business information, entrepreneurs often make decisions based on intuition alone.

Without reliable business records, organizations like HRF cannot effectively:

The Enterprise Growth App addresses this gap by creating the digital foundation for entrepreneur growth.

## 1. Executive Summary

**Purpose**

The Enterprise Growth App is the entrepreneur-facing application of the Enterprise Growth Platform. It is designed to help women entrepreneurs build stronger, more profitable businesses through simple financial management, intelligent coaching, and AI-assisted decision support.

Unlike traditional bookkeeping applications, the Enterprise Growth App is not intended to transform entrepreneurs into accountants. Instead, it serves as a practical business growth companion that enables entrepreneurs to understand their business, make informed financial decisions, and consistently reinvest profits into sustainable growth.

The application operationalizes Home Roots Foundation's Reinvest-to-Grow™ Methodology by translating daily business activities into actionable insights that help entrepreneurs retain more income, reinvest more capital, and strengthen household financial resilience.

Version 1.0 introduces the Business Journal module, the foundational component upon which future modules—including inventory purchasing, growth financing, savings, supplier integration, and business coaching—will be built.

## 2. Product Vision

**Vision Statement**

To create the world's most accessible AI-powered business growth application for first-time entrepreneurs, enabling anyone—regardless of education, literacy level, or bookkeeping experience—to confidently manage and grow a successful business.

The Enterprise Growth App should feel less like accounting software and more like an experienced business coach who is available every day.

Every interaction should help entrepreneurs answer questions such as:

- Did I make money today?
- Where did my money go?
- Can I afford to buy more inventory?
- Am I making progress?
- What should I do next?
- Am I ready for additional growth capital?

Rather than overwhelming users with accounting terminology, the application should communicate using plain language, visual indicators, and actionable recommendations.

**Long-Term Vision**

Over time, the Enterprise Growth App will evolve into an intelligent operating system for small business growth.

Ultimately, the application should become the primary interface through which entrepreneurs manage every aspect of their business.

## 3. The Problem

**Challenges Facing First-Time Entrepreneurs**

Many women entrepreneurs operating microenterprises in developing economies face similar challenges:

- Limited bookkeeping experience
- Limited formal business education
- Irregular cash flow
- Difficulty separating business and household finances
- Limited access to affordable financing
- Poor visibility into profitability
- Limited understanding of inventory performance
- Inconsistent recordkeeping
- Loss of receipts and transaction history
- Limited access to individualized business coaching

These challenges often lead entrepreneurs to make business decisions based on memory rather than reliable financial information.

Without clear visibility into business performance, entrepreneurs struggle to determine whether they are earning profits, accumulating losses, or generating sufficient cash to support future growth.

**Limitations of Traditional Bookkeeping Software**

Existing bookkeeping software is primarily designed for accountants and established businesses.

These applications often require users to understand concepts such as:

- General ledgers
- Double-entry accounting
- Journal entries
- Debits and credits
- Account reconciliation
- Chart of accounts

These concepts create unnecessary complexity for first-time entrepreneurs whose primary goal is to run and grow their businesses.

Traditional bookkeeping software focuses on producing accounting records.

The Enterprise Growth App focuses on improving business outcomes.

Accounting is the means—not the end.

## 4. Product Philosophy

Every design decision within the Enterprise Growth App shall support one or more of the following principles.

**4.1 Growth Before Accounting**

The application exists to help entrepreneurs grow profitable businesses.

Bookkeeping is valuable only because it improves business decisions.

Every feature should ultimately answer:

"How does this help the entrepreneur retain more income and reinvest more for growth?"

**4.2 Simplicity Over Complexity**

Users should never feel they are using accounting software.

Interfaces should prioritize:

- Large touch targets
- Minimal typing
- Clear language
- Visual feedback
- Guided workflows
- Conversational interactions

Complex accounting terminology should remain hidden whenever possible.

**4.3 AI-Assisted, Not AI-Dependent**

Artificial Intelligence should reduce effort rather than replace user control.

The application should:

- suggest
- organize
- classify
- explain
- educate

while always allowing users to review and confirm important financial information.

The entrepreneur remains the decision-maker.

**4.4 Mobile First**

The application will be optimized primarily for smartphones, recognizing that mobile devices are the primary computing platform for most entrepreneurs.

Tablet and web experiences may be introduced in future releases but are outside the scope of Version 1.

**4.5 Offline First**

Many entrepreneurs operate in environments with intermittent or unreliable internet connectivity.

All essential business functions must remain available without an internet connection.

Synchronization should occur automatically whenever connectivity becomes available.

No entrepreneur should lose business records because of network interruptions.

**4.6 Plain Language**

The application should communicate in language that entrepreneurs naturally use.

Examples:

Instead of:

Gross Revenue

Use:

Money Earned

Instead of:

Accounts Receivable

Use:

Money Customers Still Owe You

Instead of:

Liquidity

Use:

Cash Available

Instead of:

Net Income

Use:

Profit You Kept

Language should empower users rather than require them to learn accounting vocabulary.

**4.7 Coaching Before Compliance**

Most financial software exists to satisfy accountants, banks, or tax authorities.

The Enterprise Growth App exists to improve the entrepreneur's decisions.

Reports should therefore emphasize:

- understanding
- learning
- action
- growth

rather than merely recording history.

**4.8 Trust Through Transparency**

Users should always understand:

- why the application is making a recommendation,
- how AI arrived at a suggestion,
- what information was used,
- and whether changes require confirmation.

Trust is essential for long-term adoption.

## 8. User Experience Principles

Every screen, workflow, interaction, and feature within the Enterprise Growth App shall adhere to the following user experience principles.

These principles are mandatory design requirements rather than recommendations.

**8.1 Every Screen Must Answer a Business Question**

Entrepreneurs do not open the app to perform bookkeeping.

They open the app because they want answers.

Every screen should help answer at least one meaningful business question.

Examples:

- Did I make money today?
- What did I spend money on?
- How much cash do I have available?
- What should I buy next?
- Am I growing?
- Can I afford my next loan payment?
- What should I do today?

If a screen cannot answer one of these questions, its purpose should be reconsidered.

**8.2 Minimize Cognitive Load**

Every additional decision increases the likelihood that a user abandons the task.

Whenever possible:

- Eliminate unnecessary fields.
- Reduce typing.
- Pre-fill known information.
- Remember previous selections.
- Offer intelligent defaults.
- Use large buttons and simple layouts.

The application should think harder so the entrepreneur doesn't have to.

**8.3 Show, Don't Teach**

Instead of explaining accounting concepts, the interface should demonstrate them naturally.

Example:

Instead of displaying:

**Gross Profit Margin**

Display:

"You kept 28% of every gourde you earned."

Instead of:

**Expense Ratio**

Display:

"You spent more on transportation this week than usual."

Knowledge should emerge through use rather than instruction.

**8.4 Celebrate Progress**

Entrepreneurs should regularly experience positive reinforcement.

Examples include:

- First sale recorded.
- Seven consecutive days of bookkeeping.
- Highest weekly profit.
- Loan payment completed.
- Savings milestone reached.
- Inventory successfully restocked.
- First month of consistent records.

The application should celebrate behaviors, not just outcomes.

**8.5 Mistakes Must Be Easy to Correct**

Financial records are imperfect.

Users should always be able to:

- Edit transactions.
- Undo accidental entries.
- Correct AI suggestions.
- Replace receipt images.
- Merge duplicate records.

The system should never make entrepreneurs fear making mistakes.

**8.6 Every Interaction Should Build Trust**

Trust is essential because financial information is deeply personal.

The application should always communicate:

- What happened.
- Why it happened.
- What AI is suggesting.
- What information was changed.
- What still requires user confirmation.

No important financial action should occur without the user's knowledge.

**Product Insight: Progressive Disclosure**

A key design philosophy for the Business Journal is **progressive disclosure**. Rather than exposing every possible field at once, the app should reveal additional information only when it becomes relevant. A first-time entrepreneur recording a sale may see only the amount and payment method, while optional details like customer names or product notes remain hidden until needed. This approach keeps the interface approachable for beginners while allowing more experienced users to capture richer business information without cluttering the core experience.

## 11. AI Growth Coach

**Overview**

The AI Growth Coach is the intelligence layer of the Enterprise Growth App.

Rather than functioning as a chatbot or general-purpose assistant, the AI Growth Coach continuously analyzes business activity, identifies patterns, explains business performance, and recommends practical actions that help entrepreneurs retain more income and reinvest more capital.

The AI Growth Coach operationalizes Home Roots Foundation's Reinvest-to-Grow™ Methodology by transforming financial records into personalized business guidance.

Every recommendation should support one or more of the following objectives:

- Increase revenue
- Reduce unnecessary expenses
- Improve cash flow
- Improve inventory decisions
- Encourage consistent bookkeeping
- Strengthen loan repayment
- Build savings
- Increase business resilience
- Support long-term business growth

The AI should feel like a trusted business advisor—not an accountant.

**11.1 Core Responsibilities**

The AI Growth Coach shall:

**Explain**

Help entrepreneurs understand their business.

Example:

"Your sales increased because you sold more cooking oil this week."

**Recommend**

Suggest practical next steps.

Example:

"You have enough cash to restock rice before the weekend."

**Encourage**

Celebrate positive behaviors.

Example:

"Excellent! You've recorded your business activity every day this week."

**Educate**

Teach business concepts using real business data.

Example:

"Most successful shop owners try to keep enough cash available to purchase inventory when prices are favorable."

**Detect**

Identify unusual business patterns.

Example:

"Transportation costs are much higher than normal."

**Predict**

Estimate future outcomes.

Example:

"At your current sales rate, you'll likely need additional inventory within four days."

**11.2 Guiding Principles**

The AI Growth Coach must follow these principles.

**Advice Before Analysis**

Entrepreneurs care more about what to do than why a number changed.

Instead of displaying:

Profit ↓ 11%

Display:

"Profit was slightly lower this week. Reducing transportation expenses could help increase next week's earnings."

**Explain Every Recommendation**

Every recommendation should include:

What happened.

Why it matters.

What action to take.

Expected benefit.

Example:

Sales increased 12%.

You sold more bottled water than usual.

Consider purchasing additional inventory before the weekend.

This may help prevent stock shortages.

**Encourage Before Correcting**

When improvement is needed, acknowledge progress first.

Instead of:

"You failed to record expenses."

Use:

"Great job recording your sales. Recording expenses too will help you understand your true profit."

## 12. Intelligence Architecture

**Overview**

The Enterprise Growth App is not simply a bookkeeping application with AI features.

It is an intelligent business growth platform in which Artificial Intelligence is woven throughout the entrepreneur's experience.

The intelligence architecture defines how AI observes business activity, generates insights, recommends actions, and continuously improves while maintaining user trust.

The AI should remain largely invisible.

Entrepreneurs should feel that the application "understands" their business rather than feeling like they are interacting with a separate AI product.

**12.1 Levels of Intelligence**

The Enterprise Growth App shall implement intelligence in progressive layers.

**Level 1 — Data Capture Intelligence**

Purpose:

Reduce manual data entry.

Examples:

Automatically detect receipt amounts.

Recognize suppliers.

Recognize inventory items.

Suggest categories.

Predict transaction fields.

Autocomplete frequently entered information.

Voice transcription.

Image recognition.

Duplicate detection.

The entrepreneur remains responsible for confirming all extracted information.

**Level 2 — Pattern Recognition**

Purpose:

Understand business behavior.

Examples:

Sales trends.

Expense trends.

Seasonality.

Best-selling products.

Slow-moving inventory.

Frequent suppliers.

Customer purchasing patterns (future).

Loan repayment behavior.

Cash flow cycles.

At this level, AI begins recognizing recurring business patterns without yet recommending actions.

**Level 3 — Business Interpretation**

Purpose:

Explain what business data means.

Examples:

Sales increased.

Profit decreased.

Transportation costs rose.

Inventory is selling faster.

Cash reserves are shrinking.

Business is becoming more consistent.

This layer converts raw financial data into understandable business insights.

**Level 4 — Decision Support**

Purpose:

Recommend actions.

Examples:

Purchase additional inventory.

Reduce unnecessary spending.

Increase savings.

Prepare for loan payment.

Review pricing.

Record missing transactions.

The AI becomes a business advisor.

**Level 5 — Personalized Growth Coaching**

Purpose:

Adapt recommendations to each entrepreneur.

Examples:

A food vendor receives different recommendations than a clothing merchant.

A growing entrepreneur receives different advice than someone recovering from business disruption.

Recommendations evolve based on:

Business history

Growth stage

Goals

Inventory

Cash flow

Risk profile

Bookkeeping habits

No two entrepreneurs should experience exactly the same coaching.

**12.2 Business Memory**

One of the defining capabilities of the Enterprise Growth App is **Business Memory**.

Business Memory enables the application to remember information that reduces work and improves recommendations over time.

Examples include:

Regular suppliers.

Frequently purchased inventory.

Normal business hours.

Seasonal sales patterns.

Preferred payment methods.

Most profitable products.

Usual transportation costs.

Loan repayment habits.

Savings habits.

The entrepreneur should experience increasing simplicity as Business Memory grows.

**Functional Requirements**

**IA-001**

The system shall maintain a continuously updated business profile based on observed activity.

**IA-002**

The system shall improve future recommendations using previously confirmed user behavior.

**IA-003**

The system shall never overwrite historical financial records based solely on AI inference.

**12.3 Context Awareness**

Recommendations should always consider context.

Example:

The entrepreneur usually purchases rice every Thursday.

Today is Thursday.

Inventory is low.

Cash is available.

The AI recommends:

"You normally restock rice today. You have enough cash available to do so."

The recommendation is based on context rather than generic business advice.

**12.4 Confidence Levels**

Every AI recommendation should include an internal confidence score.

Example:

Receipt amount.

Merchant.

Date.

Automatically suggested.

75%

Expense category.

Suggested but highlighted for confirmation.

40%

Inventory quantity.

User should review carefully.

Confidence levels remain internal but determine how prominently suggestions are presented and whether confirmation is required.

**12.5 Learning Boundaries**

The AI should continuously learn from confirmed user actions.

Examples:

Corrected categories.

Edited merchants.

Frequently purchased products.

Preferred terminology.

Repeated corrections.

However, learning must never compromise data integrity.

The system should only learn from:

Confirmed transactions.

User-approved edits.

Verified receipt data.

Completed coaching interactions.

Rejected AI suggestions should not be treated as correct.

**12.6 Explainability**

Every recommendation should be explainable.

Users should always be able to ask:

"Why are you suggesting this?"

Example:

Because cooking oil has sold out three times during the past month before your normal restocking day.

Or

Because your transportation costs increased by 18% over the last three weeks.

AI recommendations should never appear mysterious.

**12.7 Human Oversight**

The Enterprise Growth Platform is built on a partnership between AI and human coaching.

The AI performs continuous analysis.

Human coaches provide judgment, empathy, cultural understanding, and personalized support.

Whenever appropriate, AI should encourage interaction with coaches rather than replacing them.

## 13. Business Rules

Business rules define how the application behaves under specific conditions and ensure consistency across future releases.

**BR-001: Financial Records Are Permanent**

Transactions may be edited or corrected, but the system shall preserve a complete audit history of every change.

Original records shall never be permanently deleted from the underlying system.

This supports transparency, coaching, impact reporting, and regulatory compliance.

**BR-002: User Confirmation Required**

AI-generated transactions, receipt extractions, and voice transcriptions shall require user confirmation before becoming official business records.

**BR-003: Time Stamps**

Every financial record shall include:

- Creation date and time.
- Last modification date and time.
- Synchronization status.
- Device identifier (when available).
- User identifier.

These metadata support synchronization, auditing, and troubleshooting.

**BR-004: Offline Priority**

Core business functions shall remain fully operational without internet connectivity.

The application shall:

- Queue transactions locally.
- Preserve transaction order.
- Prevent data loss.
- Synchronize automatically when connectivity is restored.

**BR-005: Conflict Resolution**

When synchronization conflicts occur, the system shall:

- Preserve all submitted data.
- Identify conflicting records.
- Present clear choices to the user or administrator.
- Log the final resolution.

The application should never silently overwrite financial information.

**BR-006: Currency Handling**

Version 1 supports a single operating currency per business (e.g., Haitian Gourde).

The architecture shall support multiple currencies in future releases without requiring redesign.

**BR-007: Loan Integrity**

Loan balances shall never be editable directly by entrepreneurs.

Loan balances may only change through approved administrative actions or validated repayment transactions.

This protects both entrepreneurs and the organization.

**BR-008: Data Ownership**

Entrepreneurs own the financial information they create.

Home Roots Foundation has permission to access program-related information for coaching, financing, impact measurement, and reporting in accordance with organizational policies and user agreements.

Future implementations should allow configurable permissions for partner organizations.

**BR-009: Simplicity Takes Precedence**

When there is a conflict between exposing detailed functionality and maintaining a simple user experience, the default decision shall favor simplicity.

Advanced functionality should be introduced through progressive disclosure rather than cluttering the primary interface.

**BR-010: Growth Alignment**

Every new feature proposed for the Enterprise Growth App shall answer the following design question:

**How does this feature help entrepreneurs retain more income, reinvest more capital, or grow stronger businesses?**

Features that cannot demonstrate a clear connection to these objectives should be reconsidered, deferred, or excluded from the product roadmap.

**Product Philosophy: An Adaptive System**

The Business Journal should not remain static. As entrepreneurs become more experienced, the application should gradually reveal more advanced capabilities. A first-time entrepreneur might initially see only the essential actions needed to record sales and expenses. Over time, as confidence and consistency grow, the app can introduce richer insights, more detailed reports, inventory analysis, and personalized coaching. This adaptive approach allows the software to "grow with the entrepreneur," reducing overwhelm while supporting increasingly sophisticated business management.

## 26. Security, Privacy, and Data Governance Requirements

**26.1 Purpose**

The Enterprise Growth Platform will process information that may include:

- Entrepreneur identity information
- Business information
- Financial transactions
- Receipts and business documents
- Loan information
- Repayment history
- Coaching records
- AI-generated insights
- Program participation information
- Business performance data

The platform must therefore protect information against unauthorized access, accidental loss, inappropriate use, manipulation, and disclosure.

Security and privacy are product requirements—not merely technical considerations.

**26.2 Data Governance Principles**

The platform shall follow six foundational principles.

## 1. Collect Only What Is Needed

The system should not collect personal information simply because it might become useful someday.

Every data element should have a defined purpose.

## 2. Use Data for the Stated Purpose

Data collected to provide bookkeeping, coaching, financing, or program services should not automatically be used for unrelated purposes.

## 3. User Transparency

Entrepreneurs should understand, in plain language:

- What information is collected.
- Why it is collected.
- Who may access it.
- How it supports their business.
- How it contributes to program evaluation.

## 4. Least-Privilege Access

Users should have access only to the information required for their role.

An entrepreneur should not be able to see another entrepreneur's records.

A coach should see the businesses assigned to them.

An administrator may have broader access based on their responsibilities.

## 5. Data Integrity

Financial information must be protected against unauthorized modification.

The platform must preserve a trustworthy historical record.

## 6. Security by Design

Security requirements must be incorporated during architecture and development rather than added after the product is built.

**26.3 User Authentication**

Version 1 shall support secure authentication appropriate for the operating environment.

Potential mechanisms include:

- Phone number authentication
- One-time verification codes
- Secure password/PIN
- Device-based authentication where supported

The final authentication method should balance security with usability.

A highly secure authentication system that entrepreneurs cannot use reliably is not an effective product.

**26.4 Session Security**

The application shall:

- Secure authenticated sessions.
- Automatically expire inactive sessions according to configured policies.
- Provide secure logout.
- Protect authentication credentials.
- Prevent unauthorized access if a device is lost or stolen.

The system should avoid unnecessarily frequent logins when doing so would create significant usability problems.

**26.5 Role-Based Access Control**

The Enterprise Growth Platform shall implement role-based permissions.

Initial roles may include:

**Entrepreneur**

Access:

- Own business
- Own transactions
- Own reports
- Own loan information
- Own AI insights

**Coach**

Access:

- Assigned entrepreneurs
- Business performance
- Relevant coaching information
- Program progress

**Program Administrator**

Access:

- Program participants
- Business records
- Loan information
- Portfolio information
- Program reporting

**Finance/Loan Administrator**

Access:

- Loan applications
- Disbursements
- Repayments
- Balances
- Portfolio monitoring

**System Administrator**

Access:

- System configuration
- User management
- Technical operations

System administrators should not automatically receive unrestricted access to financial or program information unless their role requires it.

**26.6 Data Encryption**

Sensitive data shall be protected using industry-standard encryption.

At minimum:

**In Transit**

Data transmitted between mobile devices, APIs, and backend systems shall use secure encrypted connections.

**At Rest**

Sensitive information stored on servers and supported local device storage shall be encrypted using appropriate platform and infrastructure controls.

**26.7 Local Device Security**

Because the application is offline-first, locally stored information represents a significant security consideration.

The mobile application shall:

- Encrypt sensitive local data.
- Protect locally cached financial information.
- Minimize unnecessary local storage.
- Secure authentication tokens.
- Remove or invalidate sensitive information when appropriate.

Receipt images should also be protected because they may contain financial or personally identifiable information.

**26.8 Financial Record Integrity**

Financial transactions require special protection.

The system shall maintain:

- Original transaction
- Modification history
- User responsible for modification
- Timestamp
- Reason for correction when appropriate

A user-facing "delete" action may remove a transaction from the normal interface, but the underlying audit record should remain preserved where appropriate.

**26.9 Audit Trail**

The platform shall maintain an auditable history of sensitive actions.

Examples include:

- Transaction creation
- Transaction modification
- Transaction reversal
- Loan disbursement
- Loan repayment adjustment
- User permission changes
- Business ownership changes
- AI-generated financial record approval
- Administrative overrides

Audit information should be protected from ordinary users.

**26.10 AI Governance**

AI introduces additional data-governance requirements.

The AI Growth Coach shall operate within clearly defined boundaries.

**AI must not:**

- Invent transactions.
- Fabricate financial information.
- Modify financial records without authorization.
- Guarantee business outcomes.
- Guarantee loan approval.
- Present guesses as facts.
- Expose another entrepreneur's information.
- Reveal confidential organizational information.

**26.11 AI Transparency**

When AI generates information, the system should distinguish between:

**Recorded Fact**

"You recorded 12 sales this week."

**Calculation**

"Your recorded profit was 3,200 HTG."

**AI Interpretation**

"Your sales appear to be increasing."

**AI Recommendation**

"Consider purchasing additional inventory."

This distinction is essential for trust.

**26.12 AI Data Minimization**

The AI system should receive only the information necessary to perform a particular task.

For example, an AI request to categorize an expense does not necessarily require access to the entrepreneur's complete profile.

This reduces unnecessary exposure of sensitive information.

**26.13 AI Training and Data Use**

The platform architecture shall distinguish between:

- **Using AI to serve an entrepreneur**, and
- **Using entrepreneur data to improve AI models.**

These are not the same thing.

Entrepreneur financial information should not automatically be used to train external or general-purpose AI models.

Any future model-training program using platform data should be subject to explicit organizational governance, appropriate consent/legal basis, data minimization, and de-identification where applicable.

**26.14 Privacy by Default**

The default system configuration should favor privacy.

Examples:

- Private business records.
- Restricted sharing.
- Minimal public information.
- Limited staff access.
- Controlled exports.

Additional sharing should require appropriate authorization.

**26.15 Data Retention**

The organization shall establish retention policies for different categories of information.

Potential categories:

- Financial transactions
- Loan records
- Receipts
- User profiles
- Coaching records
- Audit logs
- AI insights
- Program evaluation data

Retention periods should reflect organizational requirements, applicable law, contractual obligations, and legitimate program needs.

The engineering team should not hard-code arbitrary deletion policies.

Retention rules should be configurable.

**26.16 Data Export**

Entrepreneurs should eventually be able to request or receive an understandable copy of their business information.

Potential export:

- Transaction history
- Sales
- Expenses
- Receipts
- Business summaries

Administrative exports should support authorized program and funder reporting while respecting privacy controls.

**26.17 Data Correction**

Entrepreneurs must be able to correct inaccurate personal and business information.

Financial corrections should preserve historical records and auditability.

Example:

If a transaction was incorrectly entered as 500 HTG rather than 5,000 HTG:

The system should preserve evidence that the original transaction existed while clearly reflecting the corrected amount.

**26.18 Data Breach and Incident Management**

The platform should support mechanisms for detecting and responding to security incidents.

Future implementation should include:

- Security event logging
- Monitoring
- Alerting
- Incident investigation
- Access revocation
- Credential reset
- Data recovery
- Appropriate notification procedures

Specific response procedures should be established in HRF's operational security policy.

**26.19 Backup and Disaster Recovery**

Business information must not depend upon a single server, device, or database.

The platform shall implement:

- Automated backups
- Redundant storage where appropriate
- Recovery procedures
- Backup integrity verification
- Defined recovery objectives

The system should be capable of recovering from:

- Server failure
- Database corruption
- Accidental deletion
- Infrastructure outage
- Major synchronization failure

**26.20 Security Requirements for Offline Synchronization**

Offline synchronization creates a special security boundary.

The system must ensure that:

- Offline records are authenticated to the correct user/device.
- Records cannot be silently duplicated.
- Records cannot be silently overwritten.
- Synchronization traffic is encrypted.
- Conflicting changes are preserved.
- Unauthorized users cannot inject records into another business.

**26.21 Privacy-Preserving Analytics**

The organization's analytical systems should distinguish between:

**Individual-level operational data**

and

**Aggregated program-level analytics.**

For example, HRF may need to know:

"Average revenue increased 32% among the 2027 cohort."

That does not require every funder or external partner to receive individual entrepreneurs' transaction histories.

Reports should expose the minimum level of detail necessary for their intended purpose.

**26.22 Security Acceptance Criteria**

Version 1 shall not be considered production-ready until the following are demonstrated:

- Authentication works reliably.
- Unauthorized users cannot access another business.
- Sensitive data is encrypted appropriately.
- Offline data is protected.
- Audit logging works.
- Financial records cannot be silently altered.
- Synchronization does not create duplicate financial records.
- User permissions are enforced server-side.
- Backup and recovery procedures have been tested.
- AI cannot independently alter authoritative financial records.

**Strategic Principle: Trust Is a Product Feature**

For the Enterprise Growth Platform, security cannot be treated as invisible infrastructure alone.

**Trust itself is part of the user experience.**

An entrepreneur is more likely to consistently record sales, share financial information, engage with AI recommendations, and use financing tools when she believes:

"My information is safe."

"The numbers are accurate."

"The organization cannot secretly change my records."

"The AI will not make decisions for me."

"I understand how my information is being used."

That trust will become increasingly important as the platform expands from bookkeeping into financing, purchasing, savings, and other financial services.

## 27. Non-Functional Requirements

The next layer of the specification defines how well the system must operate—not just what it must do.

The Enterprise Growth App shall be designed for:

- Reliability
- Speed
- Offline resilience
- Scalability
- Maintainability
- Security
- Observability
- Accessibility

**27.1 Performance**

The application should feel fast even on lower-cost Android devices and weaker networks.

Priority actions should load quickly.

Examples:

- Opening the app
- Recording a sale
- Recording an expense
- Viewing recent transactions

The user should not have to wait for cloud processing to complete before completing basic business activities.

**27.2 Offline Performance**

Core Business Journal functions should remain responsive while offline.

The application should not repeatedly display connection errors when connectivity is unavailable.

Instead, it should quietly operate locally and synchronize later.

**27.3 Reliability**

The system should prioritize financial data integrity above convenience.

If there is uncertainty during synchronization, the system should preserve the data and resolve the conflict rather than risk losing information.

**27.4 Scalability**

The architecture should support future growth from:

**50 entrepreneurs**

→ **500**

→ **5,000**

→ **50,000+**

without requiring fundamental redesign of the core data model.

The system should also support multiple program cohorts and eventually multiple geographic regions.

**27.5 Maintainability**

Future engineers must be able to understand and modify the system without depending on the original developer.

Requirements should include:

- Clear code organization
- Documented APIs
- Version-controlled infrastructure
- Automated testing
- Database migration strategy
- Configuration management
- Technical documentation

**27.6 Observability**

The engineering team must be able to determine:

- Is the app working?
- Are transactions synchronizing?
- Are AI services responding?
- Are users experiencing errors?
- Are receipt scans failing?
- Are specific devices having problems?

The system should provide appropriate logs, metrics, and monitoring without exposing sensitive user information unnecessarily.

**27.7 Graceful Failure**

When a non-critical service fails, the core business application should continue operating whenever possible.

For example:

If AI receipt classification is temporarily unavailable:

The entrepreneur should still be able to record the expense manually.

If AI recommendations are unavailable:

The Business Journal should continue functioning.

If the internet is unavailable:

The entrepreneur should continue recording transactions.

This is essential.

**AI is an enhancement to the business system—not a single point of failure.**

**Strategic Architecture Principle**

The Enterprise Growth App should be designed so that **the entrepreneur's business never stops because the software has a problem**.

That principle should guide the engineering architecture throughout development.

## 15. Testing & Quality Assurance Requirements

**15.1 Purpose**

Testing shall verify that the Enterprise Growth App is reliable, understandable, secure, and useful under the real-world conditions in which HRF entrepreneurs operate.

Version 1 shall not be considered complete merely because all planned screens and features have been implemented.

The product must demonstrate that entrepreneurs can reliably use the Business Journal to record business activity, understand their financial performance, and operate the application under conditions including intermittent connectivity, low-cost devices, multilingual interaction, and limited bookkeeping experience.

Quality assurance shall therefore evaluate five dimensions:

- **Functional correctness**
- **Data integrity**
- **Usability**
- **Reliability and offline resilience**
- **Security and privacy**

AI-powered capabilities require additional evaluation for accuracy, consistency, explainability, and appropriate human oversight.

**15.2 Testing Philosophy**

The Enterprise Growth App should be tested under the conditions in which it will actually be used—not only under ideal laboratory conditions.

Testing should include:

- Realistic Android devices
- Weak and intermittent connectivity
- Offline periods
- Cash-based transactions
- Haitian Creole interaction
- French interaction where supported
- Voice input
- Receipt images of varying quality
- Users with limited bookkeeping experience
- Interrupted workflows
- Synchronization failures
- Incorrect user inputs

The product should be **failure-tolerant**.

When something goes wrong, the system should preserve the entrepreneur's information whenever possible and provide a clear path to recovery.

**15.3 Testing Levels**

Testing shall occur at multiple levels.

**Level 1 — Unit Testing**

Individual functions and components shall be tested independently.

Examples:

- Transaction calculations
- Profit calculations
- Loan balance calculations
- Category assignment
- Date handling
- Currency handling
- Data validation

**Level 2 — Integration Testing**

Verify that different system components work together.

Examples:

Mobile application → API

API → database

Receipt scanner → transaction creation

Transaction → dashboard

Transaction → analytics

Loan system → entrepreneur dashboard

Offline storage → synchronization service

AI service → review workflow

**Level 3 — End-to-End Testing**

Test complete entrepreneur workflows from beginning to end.

Example:

Record Sale

→ transaction stored

→ dashboard updated

→ profit recalculated

→ synchronization completed

→ report updated

→ AI insight generated.

**Level 4 — User Acceptance Testing**

Realistic users should test the application using representative business scenarios.

Testing should include entrepreneurs with different:

- Business types
- Digital experience
- Literacy levels
- Languages
- Transaction volumes
- Connectivity conditions

**15.4 Functional Testing**

Every Version 1 functional requirement shall have corresponding test cases.

Core functionality shall include:

**Business Journal**

- Record sale
- Record expense
- Edit transaction
- Correct transaction
- View transaction history
- Search/filter transactions
- Add notes
- Attach receipts
- View transaction status

**Receipt Processing**

- Capture receipt
- Extract information
- Review extracted information
- Correct extracted information
- Confirm transaction
- Reject transaction

**Voice**

- Record voice input
- Transcribe speech
- Identify transaction intent
- Extract transaction information
- Ask clarification questions
- Confirm transaction

**Dashboard**

- Display business summary
- Calculate sales
- Calculate expenses
- Calculate profit
- Display cash information
- Display relevant insights

**Reports**

- Generate reports
- Change reporting periods
- Display historical information
- Explain trends

**Loan Information**

- Display loan balance
- Display repayment information
- Display payment history

**15.5 Financial Calculation Testing**

Financial calculations require particularly rigorous testing.

The system shall be tested for:

- Addition and subtraction
- Revenue calculations
- Expense calculations
- Profit calculations
- Cash movement
- Loan balances
- Repayment calculations
- Rounding
- Currency precision
- Date boundaries
- Monthly/weekly reporting periods

Example:

If recorded sales equal 10,000 HTG and recorded business expenses equal 6,500 HTG:

**Profit = 3,500 HTG**

The result must remain consistent across:

- Dashboard
- Reports
- AI explanations
- Administrative systems

There shall be one authoritative calculation source rather than separate calculations performed independently by different interfaces.

**15.6 Offline Testing**

Offline functionality is a release-critical capability.

Testing shall simulate:

**Scenario A — Completely Offline**

User records several transactions with no internet connection.

Expected result:

All transactions remain available locally and are not lost.

**Scenario B — Intermittent Connectivity**

Connectivity repeatedly switches between available and unavailable.

Expected result:

Transactions are preserved and synchronized without duplication.

**Scenario C — Application Closed During Sync**

Expected result:

Synchronization resumes safely when the application is reopened.

**Scenario D — Device Restart**

Expected result:

Unsynchronized transactions remain available after device restart.

**Scenario E — Duplicate Synchronization Attempt**

Expected result:

The backend recognizes previously synchronized records and does not create duplicates.

**15.7 Synchronization Testing**

Synchronization shall be tested for:

- Duplicate prevention
- Conflict detection
- Transaction ordering
- Timestamp integrity
- Partial synchronization
- Failed synchronization
- Retry behavior
- Authentication expiration
- Server errors

The system shall favor **data preservation over silent resolution** when uncertainty exists.

**15.8 Receipt and Document Testing**

Receipt processing should be tested using a representative dataset containing:

- Clear receipts
- Blurry receipts
- Folded receipts
- Poor lighting
- Handwritten information
- Printed receipts
- Different layouts
- Different languages
- Partial receipts
- Receipts with multiple products
- Receipts with unclear totals

The system should measure:

- Amount extraction accuracy
- Date extraction accuracy
- Merchant recognition
- Item recognition
- Category suggestion accuracy

AI extraction shall never automatically create a financial record when confidence is insufficient.

**15.9 Voice Testing**

Voice functionality shall be tested with realistic speech patterns rather than only scripted sentences.

Testing should include:

- Different accents
- Different speaking speeds
- Background noise
- Informal language
- Mixed-language speech where supported
- Different microphones
- Short statements
- Long statements
- Incomplete statements

The system should distinguish between:

**What the user said**

and

**What the AI believes the user meant.**

When uncertainty is significant, the system should ask for confirmation.

**15.10 Multilingual Testing**

Translations shall be evaluated for:

- Accuracy
- Meaning
- Natural phrasing
- Cultural appropriateness
- Consistency
- Financial terminology

Haitian Creole should be evaluated by fluent Haitian Creole speakers familiar with everyday business language.

Literal translation from English should not be considered sufficient.

AI-generated explanations must also be reviewed for meaning and appropriateness.

**15.11 Usability Testing**

The application should be tested with entrepreneurs who have little or no bookkeeping experience.

Key questions include:

- Can the user understand what to do?
- Can the user complete a transaction without assistance?
- Does the user understand the confirmation?
- Does the user understand profit?
- Does the user know when a transaction has been saved?
- Can the user recover from a mistake?
- Does the user understand AI recommendations?
- Does the user know what action to take next?

**15.12 Usability Acceptance Criteria**

For core workflows, pilot users should be able to independently complete:

- Record a sale.
- Record an expense.
- Scan and confirm a receipt.
- Correct an incorrect transaction.
- View recent transactions.
- Determine weekly sales.
- Determine weekly profit.
- View loan balance where applicable.
- Complete a basic weekly review.

The majority of first-time users should be able to complete core workflows without requiring staff intervention.

Exact usability thresholds should be established during pilot testing rather than assumed before baseline user testing.

**15.13 AI Quality Assurance**

AI functionality shall be tested separately from conventional software functionality.

Testing shall evaluate:

**Accuracy**

Did the AI correctly identify the transaction?

**Completeness**

Did it capture all important information?

**Consistency**

Does the same input produce reasonably consistent results?

**Explainability**

Can the recommendation be explained using actual business data?

**Safety**

Does the AI avoid inventing information?

**Appropriate Uncertainty**

Does the AI recognize when it does not know?

**15.14 AI Hallucination Testing**

The system shall be tested against prompts designed to cause unsupported conclusions.

Examples:

"How much did I sell last Tuesday?"

when no Tuesday data exists.

The system should respond that the available records do not provide enough information.

It should not invent a number.

Similarly:

"Why did my profit fall?"

If the data does not establish a reason, the system should communicate uncertainty rather than present speculation as fact.

**15.15 AI Financial Integrity Testing**

AI must never be treated as the authoritative source for financial calculations.

The system shall derive authoritative financial values from validated transaction data.

AI may:

- Explain
- Summarize
- Classify
- Recommend
- Identify patterns

AI may not independently establish authoritative financial facts.

For example:

The accounting/data layer determines:

**Profit = 3,500 HTG**

The AI may explain:

"You kept 3,500 HTG after your recorded business expenses."

**15.16 Security Testing**

Security testing shall include:

- Authentication testing
- Authorization testing
- Role-permission testing
- API security testing
- Data encryption verification
- Local storage security
- Session management
- Audit log protection
- Data export controls
- Attempted cross-business access
- Device-loss scenarios

A user must never be able to manipulate the application to access another entrepreneur's financial information.

**15.17 Performance Testing**

Performance testing shall evaluate:

- Application startup
- Dashboard loading
- Transaction creation
- Transaction search
- Report generation
- Receipt processing
- Synchronization
- AI response times

Testing should include lower-cost Android devices and weaker network conditions.

Performance requirements should be established as measurable targets during engineering implementation.

**15.18 Regression Testing**

Every significant release shall be tested against previously validated functionality.

A new feature must not unintentionally break:

- Transaction recording
- Calculations
- Offline mode
- Synchronization
- Reports
- Authentication
- Loan information

Automated regression testing should be implemented for high-risk and frequently used workflows.

**15.19 Data Integrity Testing**

The following must be tested:

- No transaction loss
- No duplicate transactions
- No unexplained balance changes
- No unauthorized modifications
- Correct historical records
- Correct timestamps
- Correct synchronization status
- Correct report totals

Data integrity failures involving financial information shall be treated as release-blocking defects.

**15.20 Defect Classification**

Defects should be classified by severity.

**Critical**

Causes:

- Financial data loss
- Unauthorized financial access
- Incorrect authoritative loan balance
- Security breach
- Corruption of financial records

**Release blocking.**

**High**

Prevents a core business workflow from functioning reliably.

Examples:

- Cannot record sales
- Offline transactions disappear
- Synchronization creates duplicates

**Normally release blocking.**

**Medium**

Significant inconvenience but workaround exists.

**Low**

Cosmetic or minor usability issues that do not materially affect business operations.

**15.21 Pilot Readiness Gate**

Version 1 should not enter an entrepreneur pilot until:

- Critical defects are resolved.
- High-severity defects affecting core workflows are resolved.
- Financial calculations have been validated.
- Offline synchronization has been tested.
- Security controls have been tested.
- Core workflows have passed user acceptance testing.
- Haitian Creole experience has been reviewed.
- AI safeguards have been tested.
- Backup and recovery procedures have been validated.
- Monitoring and error reporting are operational.

**15.22 Pilot Feedback Loop**

Testing does not end when the pilot begins.

The pilot should establish a structured feedback loop:

**Entrepreneur behavior**

↓

**Observed friction**

↓

**User feedback**

↓

**Product analytics**

↓

**Engineering diagnosis**

↓

**Product improvement**

↓

**Retesting**

↓

**Next release**

The objective is not simply to eliminate software bugs.

The objective is to discover where the product fails to fit naturally into entrepreneurs' actual business routines.

**15.23 Definition of Quality**

For Version 1, quality means more than technical correctness.

The product is high quality when an entrepreneur can reliably say:

"I can record what happens in my business."

"I understand what the numbers mean."

"I can use the app even when the internet is unreliable."

"I trust the information."

"The app helps me make better business decisions."

That standard should guide both engineering and product decisions.

## 16. Product Success Metrics & Key Performance Indicators

**16.1 Purpose**

The Enterprise Growth App shall be evaluated based on whether it creates meaningful value for entrepreneurs and strengthens the Enterprise Growth Platform.

Success shall be measured across four levels:

- **Product Adoption** — Are entrepreneurs using the application?
- **Data Quality & Business Discipline** — Are entrepreneurs consistently recording reliable business activity?
- **Decision Support & Engagement** — Is the application helping entrepreneurs understand and manage their businesses?
- **Business and Program Outcomes** — Is improved business management contributing to stronger enterprise and financing outcomes?

The platform shall avoid optimizing for engagement metrics that do not create meaningful business value.

For example, increasing the number of notifications opened is not inherently a product success if those notifications do not improve business management.

The primary measurement framework shall be:

**Adoption → Usage → Data Quality → Insight → Action → Business Outcome**

Entrepreneur joins

↓

Uses Business Journal

↓

Creates reliable business records

↓

Platform identifies patterns

↓

Entrepreneur receives useful insight

↓

Entrepreneur takes action

↓

Business performance improves

↓

Longitudinal data supports better coaching and financing

This creates the product's core learning loop.

**16.3 Product Adoption Metrics**

The platform shall track:

**Registered Entrepreneurs**

Number of entrepreneurs with active accounts.

**Activated Entrepreneurs**

Number of entrepreneurs who have completed the minimum onboarding process and successfully recorded at least one business activity.

**Activation Rate**

Percentage of registered entrepreneurs who become active users.

**Monthly Active Entrepreneurs**

Number of entrepreneurs who meaningfully interact with the application during a month.

**Weekly Active Entrepreneurs**

Number of entrepreneurs who meaningfully interact with the application during a week.

**Retention**

Percentage of entrepreneurs continuing to use the application after:

- 7 days
- 30 days
- 90 days
- 180 days

Retention should be measured by meaningful business activity rather than simply opening the application.

The primary objective of the Business Journal is consistent capture of business activity.

The platform shall measure:

**Transactions Recorded**

Number of transactions recorded per entrepreneur.

**Transactions per Active Entrepreneur**

Average number of transactions recorded during a defined period.

**Recording Frequency**

How consistently entrepreneurs record business activity.

Potential measurement:

- Daily
- Weekly
- Monthly

**Active Bookkeeping Days**

Number of days during which an entrepreneur records at least one business activity.

**Bookkeeping Consistency**

Percentage of expected business periods in which the entrepreneur maintains records.

This metric should eventually become more sophisticated than simply counting logins.

The platform shall monitor:

**Transaction Completion Rate**

Percentage of initiated transactions successfully completed.

Percentage of AI-generated transactions reviewed and confirmed by users.

Percentage of AI-generated transactions requiring user correction.

This helps identify areas where AI performance needs improvement.

**Receipt Extraction Accuracy**

Accuracy of extracted:

- Amount
- Date
- Merchant
- Items
- Category

**Voice Interpretation Accuracy**

Percentage of voice transactions correctly interpreted without material correction.

**Duplicate Transaction Rate**

Percentage of transactions identified as duplicates.

The target should approach zero for synchronized authoritative records.

**Data Correction Rate**

Frequency with which previously recorded transactions require correction.

This can reveal both user-interface problems and data-entry difficulties.

**16.6 Offline Reliability Metrics**

- Offline transactions successfully preserved
- Offline transactions successfully synchronized
- Synchronization failures
- Duplicate synchronization events
- Unresolved synchronization conflicts
- Data loss incidents

How often AI correctly classifies transaction types/categories.

Accuracy of receipt and document extraction.

Percentage of voice requests correctly interpreted.

## 17. Credit Intelligence & Growth Capital Readiness

**17.1 Purpose**

The Enterprise Growth Platform is intended to eventually support responsible, graduated growth financing for entrepreneurs participating in the Reinvest-to-Grow™ Methodology.

Version 1 shall **not implement a fully automated credit-scoring or automated lending-decision system**.

However, the Version 1 data architecture shall intentionally capture the business, financial, behavioral, and repayment information necessary to support future:

- Growth Capital Readiness assessment
- Credit intelligence
- Risk modeling
- Financing recommendations
- Graduated financing limits
- Repeat financing
- Enterprise Growth Pathways

The objective is to build the foundation now without creating false precision before sufficient data exists.

**17.2 Strategic Principle**

Traditional credit scoring primarily asks:

"Will this borrower repay?"

The Enterprise Growth Platform should eventually be capable of answering a broader question:

**"Is this business ready for additional productive capital, how much capital can it reasonably absorb, and what financing structure is most appropriate?"**

This distinction is central to the Reinvest-to-Grow™ Methodology.

The platform is designed to finance **business growth**, not simply extend loans.

**17.3 Version 1 Boundary**

Version 1 shall:

**Capture**

- Business activity
- Revenue
- Expenses
- Profit
- Cash movement
- Inventory activity where supported
- Business history
- Loan information where applicable
- Repayment behavior
- Savings information where integrated
- Coaching and program participation data where appropriate

**Display**

Basic business health indicators.

**Analyze**

Historical business activity.

**Prepare**

Data structures for future growth-capital readiness assessment.

Version 1 shall not:

- Automatically approve loans.
- Automatically decline loans.
- Assign an authoritative credit score.
- Automatically determine financing amounts.
- Automatically change loan terms.
- Make lending decisions without authorized human oversight.

**17.4 Growth Capital Readiness**

Before introducing a formal score, the platform should develop a **Growth Capital Readiness framework**.

The framework should assess whether an entrepreneur demonstrates characteristics associated with productive use of additional capital.

Potential dimensions include:

- Business activity
- Financial health
- Business discipline
- Repayment behavior
- Growth trajectory
- Capital utilization
- Business stability

**17.5 Business Activity Indicators**

Potential indicators include:

- Length of business operating history
- Frequency of sales
- Consistency of transaction recording
- Revenue stability
- Revenue growth
- Seasonal patterns
- Business activity interruptions

The system should distinguish between:

**Low business activity**

and

**Low record-keeping activity.**

An entrepreneur who fails to record transactions may have a healthy business that is simply not being documented.

This distinction is important before using data in any financing decision.

**17.6 Financial Health Indicators**

Potential indicators include:

**Revenue**

- Average revenue
- Revenue trend
- Revenue volatility
- Seasonal patterns

**Expenses**

- Average expenses
- Expense volatility
- Major expense categories
- Expense-to-revenue relationship

**Profitability**

- Average profit
- Profit consistency
- Profit trend
- Estimated operating margin

**Cash Flow**

- Cash inflows
- Cash outflows
- Cash-flow volatility
- Cash availability

These measures should be based on validated transaction data wherever possible.

**17.7 Business Discipline Indicators**

Business discipline is potentially important to HRF's model because consistent management behavior may be informative even when formal credit history is limited.

Potential indicators:

- Consistent bookkeeping
- Timely transaction recording
- Receipt documentation
- Regular business reviews
- Savings behavior
- Inventory management
- Separation of business and household funds where supported
- Responsiveness to coaching

These indicators should never be treated as automatic proof of creditworthiness.

They are signals that require contextual interpretation.

**17.8 Repayment Behavior**

Where an entrepreneur has previously received financing through HRF or an integrated financing partner, the platform may track:

- Amount financed
- Payment schedule
- Payments made
- Payment timing
- Missed payments
- Partial payments
- Days past due
- Loan completion
- Repeat financing

Repayment history should become one of the strongest data sources for future risk modeling.

**17.9 Growth Trajectory**

The platform should assess whether the business demonstrates evidence of growth.

Potential indicators:

- Revenue growth
- Profit growth
- Customer growth where measurable
- Inventory growth
- Business expansion
- New products
- Additional employees
- Increased purchasing capacity
- Increased savings

Growth should be evaluated relative to the business's starting point.

A small business growing steadily may demonstrate stronger growth potential than a larger but stagnant business.

**17.10 Capital Utilization**

As the platform evolves, it should track how financing is used.

Potential categories:

- Inventory
- Equipment
- Working capital
- Business expansion
- Transportation
- Product diversification
- Other approved business purposes

The platform should eventually compare:

**Capital deployed**

with

**Business activity following deployment.**

This can help HRF understand whether financing is producing productive business outcomes.

**17.11 Financing Progression**

The eventual platform should support a graduated financing pathway.

Illustrative model:

**Entry Capital**

↓

**Growth Capital**

↓

**Expanded Growth Capital**

↓

**Enterprise Capital**

Progression should be based on demonstrated business performance and financing readiness rather than simply time enrolled in the program.

**17.12 Growth Capital Readiness Levels**

Future versions may introduce readiness levels such as:

**Level 1 — Building a Business Record**

The entrepreneur is establishing reliable business records.

**Level 2 — Demonstrating Business Activity**

The business demonstrates consistent commercial activity.

**Level 3 — Demonstrating Financial Stability**

Revenue, expenses, and profitability show sufficient consistency for deeper analysis.

**Level 4 — Demonstrating Financing Readiness**

The entrepreneur demonstrates sufficient business performance and financing discipline for consideration of additional capital.

**Level 5 — Demonstrating Growth Capacity**

The business demonstrates evidence that additional capital could reasonably support expansion.

These levels are conceptual and shall not become financing rules until validated through HRF's experience and data.

**17.13 Future Growth Capital Score**

A future version may introduce a quantitative score.

Possible dimensions:

| **Dimension** | **Potential Weighting** |
| --- | --- |
| Business activity | TBD |
| Financial health | TBD |
| Repayment behavior | TBD |
| Business discipline | TBD |
| Growth trajectory | TBD |
| Capital utilization | TBD |
| Business stability | TBD |

**No weights shall be established in Version 1 without empirical validation.**

The platform should first collect the underlying data and determine which indicators actually predict useful outcomes.

**17.14 Why the Score Should Be Developed Empirically**

HRF should not assume that conventional credit-scoring variables will perform well in Haiti's informal and cash-heavy business environment.

The organization should eventually investigate:

Which observable business behaviors predict repayment?

and:

Which business characteristics predict productive use of additional capital?

For example, HRF may discover that:

- Revenue consistency
- Inventory turnover
- Prior repayment behavior
- Profit stability

are more useful predictors than conventional credit variables that are unavailable to many entrepreneurs.

**17.15 Human-in-the-Loop Lending**

Even after predictive models become available, financing decisions should initially remain subject to human review.

The platform may eventually recommend:

**Growth Capital Readiness: Strong**

**Suggested financing range: 25,000–35,000 HTG**

**Primary supporting factors:**

- Consistent revenue
- Positive profit trend
- Strong repayment history
- Stable inventory activity

The authorized financing officer or committee would then make the final decision.

This creates accountability and allows HRF to identify cases where the model is wrong.

**17.16 Explainability Requirements**

Any future financing-readiness model should provide understandable reasons for its assessment.

The system should not simply display:

Score: 78

It should explain:

"Your readiness is strong because your sales have remained consistent, your profit has increased, and your previous loan payments were made on time."

If negative factors exist:

"Your readiness is currently limited because your recent cash flow has been inconsistent."

Entrepreneurs and authorized staff should be able to understand the principal factors influencing the assessment.

**17.17 No Hidden Penalization**

The platform should not allow opaque behavioral signals to silently reduce financing eligibility.

If a variable influences a financing assessment, HRF should be able to identify and justify its use.

This is particularly important for:

- AI-generated classifications
- Bookkeeping frequency
- Voice usage
- App engagement
- Geographic information
- Household information

A user should not be penalized simply because she:

- Uses voice instead of touch.
- Has poor connectivity.
- Has an older phone.
- Records transactions less frequently because of her business type.

**17.18 Fairness and Bias Monitoring**

Any future credit model shall be evaluated for unintended disparities.

HRF should monitor model performance across relevant groups and business contexts where legally and ethically appropriate.

The organization should examine whether the model systematically disadvantages entrepreneurs because of:

- Business sector
- Business size
- Location
- Digital access
- Language
- Transaction-recording behavior
- Other characteristics unrelated to actual financing risk

A model that predicts repayment accurately overall but systematically excludes viable entrepreneurs is not sufficient for HRF's mission.

**17.19 Alternative Data Governance**

The platform may eventually use "alternative data" to supplement conventional financial information.

Potential examples:

- Business transaction history
- Inventory activity
- Savings behavior
- Repayment behavior
- Business longevity
- Cash-flow patterns

However, not every available data point should automatically become a credit variable.

HRF shall establish governance criteria for determining:

- Whether a variable is relevant.
- Whether it is reliable.
- Whether it is fair.
- Whether it is explainable.
- Whether its use is appropriate.
- Whether its predictive value has been validated.

**17.20 Minimum Data Requirements Before Formal Scoring**

A formal predictive credit model should not be deployed simply because sufficient software data exists.

Before implementation, HRF should establish:

- Adequate historical observations
- Reliable transaction data
- Sufficient repayment outcomes
- Standardized definitions
- Consistent data collection
- Model validation
- Out-of-sample testing
- Bias/fairness testing
- Human review procedures
- Monitoring procedures

The exact sample size and statistical thresholds should be determined with qualified data-science and risk-management expertise.

**17.21 Credit Model Monitoring**

Once a model is implemented, it must be continuously monitored.

The platform should track:

- Prediction accuracy
- Repayment outcomes
- Default rates
- Delinquency
- False positives
- False negatives
- Model drift
- Population changes
- Performance across cohorts

The model should be recalibrated or retired when its predictive performance deteriorates materially.

**17.22 Relationship to Reinvest-to-Grow™**

Credit intelligence should reinforce—not replace—the Reinvest-to-Grow™ Methodology.

The sequence should be:

**Business Activity**

→

**Business Visibility**

→

**Business Coaching**

→

**Business Improvement**

→

**Demonstrated Growth**

→

**Responsible Growth Capital**

→

**Further Growth**

This creates a fundamentally different philosophy from:

**Loan → repayment → repeat loan.**

The platform should help HRF understand whether capital is actually helping entrepreneurs build stronger businesses.

**17.23 Future Financing Recommendation Engine**

A future version may combine:

- Growth Capital Readiness
- Business performance
- Prior financing
- Repayment behavior
- Capital utilization
- Business goals

to generate financing recommendations.

Example:

**Recommended next step**

Your business appears ready for additional working capital. Based on your recent sales and inventory patterns, additional inventory financing may be more appropriate than a general-purpose loan.

This is a future capability—not a Version 1 requirement.

**17.24 Future Dynamic Financing**

At maturity, the Enterprise Growth Platform may support financing that evolves with business performance.

For example:

**Initial financing:** 20,000 HTG

↓

Strong repayment + business growth

↓

**Next financing:** 30,000 HTG

↓

Strong repayment + increased business capacity

↓

**Next financing:** 50,000 HTG

The actual amounts and rules must be determined empirically and operationally.

The important architectural requirement is that the platform should not assume that every entrepreneur requires the same fixed financing amount.
