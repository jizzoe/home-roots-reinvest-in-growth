# Reinvest-to-Grow™ Methodology Summary

**HOME ROOTS FOUNDATION**

**Reinvest-to-Grow™ Methodology**

**Model Summary v2.0**

**An Integrated Enterprise Growth Model for Women Entrepreneurs in Haiti**

## 1. One-Sentence Summary

**Home Roots Foundation's Reinvest-to-Grow™ Methodology helps women entrepreneurs retain more of the income their businesses generate and systematically reinvest it into business growth through purchasing power, growth capital, business support, and digital business intelligence—strengthening enterprises, household financial resilience, and pathways out of poverty.**

## 2. Home Roots Foundation

**Mission**

Home Roots Foundation (HRF) partners with women entrepreneurs and their families in Haiti to build stronger businesses, increase economic resilience, and create pathways toward greater household stability and opportunity.

HRF's enterprise work is centered on a simple proposition:

**When entrepreneurs can retain more of their business income and reinvest it productively, their businesses have a greater opportunity to become stronger, more stable, and capable of generating lasting household income.**

## 3. The Problem

Many women in Haiti are already economically active. They operate retail businesses, food businesses, service enterprises, and other micro and small enterprises.

The challenge is not simply a lack of entrepreneurial effort.

Many businesses face structural constraints that make sustained growth difficult.

**Constraint 1 — Limited Purchasing Power**

Small businesses frequently purchase inventory in small quantities and at relatively high prices.

This can result in:

- Higher input costs.
- Lower margins.
- Less retained profit.
- Limited ability to compete.

**Constraint 2 — Limited Working Capital**

Entrepreneurs may lack sufficient capital to:

- Purchase inventory in larger quantities.
- Expand product offerings.
- Respond to customer demand.
- Take advantage of favorable purchasing opportunities.

**Constraint 3 — Weak Business Information**

Many small businesses operate without reliable records of:

- Sales.
- Expenses.
- Cash movement.
- Profit.
- Inventory.
- Business trends.

Without this information, entrepreneurs may have difficulty knowing which activities are actually generating profit or making informed reinvestment decisions.

It also makes it more difficult for support organizations and financial providers to understand business performance.

**Constraint 4 — Household Financial Pressure**

Business income and household income are often closely intertwined.

When household needs arise, entrepreneurs may have to withdraw money that would otherwise have remained in the business.

This can reduce working capital and slow enterprise growth.

HRF does not view this simply as a matter of "poor financial discipline."

It is often a consequence of operating with limited financial buffers.

The model therefore seeks to increase the amount of income that can be **retained within the business while also strengthening household financial resilience.**

## 4. The Core Insight

Many development interventions focus primarily on increasing income.

HRF focuses on an additional question:

**How much of the income generated by the business can the entrepreneur retain and productively reinvest?**

The Reinvest-to-Grow™ Methodology is built around the idea that sustainable enterprise growth requires more than capital alone.

An entrepreneur needs a system that helps her:

**Buy more efficiently → operate better → retain more → reinvest → grow.**

The methodology therefore combines complementary forms of support rather than treating financing, purchasing, training, and business management as separate interventions.

## 5. The Reinvest-to-Grow™ Methodology

The methodology currently consists of four interconnected growth levers:

## 1. Purchasing Power

Help entrepreneurs access inventory and business inputs at more favorable economics.

## 2. Growth Capital

Provide appropriately structured financing that can be used for productive business growth.

## 3. Business Support

Provide practical training, coaching, and support that help entrepreneurs manage and grow their businesses.

## 4. Business Intelligence

Help entrepreneurs capture and understand business activity while creating reliable information that can improve coaching, decision-making, measurement, and eventually financing readiness.

Together, these components create a reinforcing enterprise growth system.

## 7. Component 1 — Purchasing Power

**Enterprise Supply Hub**

HRF's Enterprise Supply Hub is designed to aggregate purchasing demand and provide entrepreneurs with access to lower-cost inventory and business supplies.

Potential products include commonly traded goods such as:

- Rice.
- Cooking oil.
- Sugar.
- Soap and detergents.
- Beverages.
- Cosmetics.
- Packaging materials.
- Phone credit.
- Other frequently purchased business inputs.

The Supply Hub allows participating entrepreneurs to access purchasing economics that may be difficult to achieve individually.

**Entrepreneur Value**

The objective is to:

- Reduce inventory costs.
- Improve gross margins.
- Increase retained earnings.
- Improve inventory availability.
- Strengthen purchasing efficiency.

**HRF Value**

The Supply Hub is also intended to become an earned-revenue-generating operating unit.

Its commercial activity can help contribute toward local operational sustainability while directly supporting entrepreneur growth.

## 8. Component 2 — Growth Capital

HRF provides or facilitates financing designed around the productive needs of growing enterprises.

Financing may support:

- Inventory expansion.
- Working capital.
- Product expansion.
- Business equipment.
- Other productive investments.

The purpose of growth capital is not simply to increase access to loans.

The objective is:

**To provide capital that entrepreneurs can productively deploy and reinvest in ways that strengthen their businesses.**

As the model develops, financing pathways can become increasingly informed by actual business performance and longitudinal enterprise data.

## 9. Component 3 — Business Support

Capital and purchasing advantages do not automatically produce enterprise growth.

Entrepreneurs also need practical support.

HRF's business support model may include:

- Business training.
- Bookkeeping/business recordkeeping.
- Business planning.
- Inventory management.
- Pricing and margin management.
- Reinvestment planning.
- Financial decision-making.
- Coaching.
- Market-based support.

Support should be practical, lightweight, and scalable.

The objective is not to make entrepreneurs accountants or business-school graduates.

The objective is to help them make better decisions in the businesses they already operate.

## 10. Component 4 — Business Intelligence

Business intelligence is becoming an increasingly important component of the Reinvest-to-Grow™ Methodology.

Historically, many entrepreneurs have operated without reliable longitudinal records.

HRF is building digital infrastructure to change this.

**Enterprise Growth App**

The Enterprise Growth App is the entrepreneur-facing digital component of the Enterprise Growth Platform.

Version 1 begins with the **Business Journal**, enabling entrepreneurs to record and understand:

- Sales.
- Expenses.
- Cash movement.
- Profit.
- Receipts.
- Business activity.

The application is designed to be:

- Mobile-first.
- Simple enough for entrepreneurs without bookkeeping training.
- Offline-capable.
- Multilingual.
- Assisted by AI where useful.

## 11. Why Business Data Matters

The purpose of business data is not simply recordkeeping.

Over time, reliable business information can help answer:

- Is the business growing?
- What products generate the strongest margins?
- How consistent is revenue?
- How much does the entrepreneur reinvest?
- Where are costs increasing?
- How does financing affect business performance?
- Which forms of support produce better outcomes?

For HRF, this creates the potential to move from generalized program support toward **increasingly data-informed enterprise support.**

For entrepreneurs, it creates greater visibility into their own businesses.

## 13. The Entrepreneur Journey

The long-term objective is to help entrepreneurs progress through a pathway from economic activity toward stronger and more resilient enterprises.

**Stage 1 — Enter**

Entrepreneur is operating an existing business and enters HRF's enterprise growth system.

↓

**Stage 2 — Stabilize**

Entrepreneur improves basic business practices, recordkeeping, purchasing, and working-capital management.

↓

**Stage 3 — Strengthen**

Entrepreneur improves margins, builds business discipline, and develops more reliable cash flow.

↓

**Stage 4 — Grow**

Entrepreneur uses retained earnings, purchasing advantages, and appropriate growth capital to expand the business.

↓

**Stage 5 — Sustain**

Entrepreneur operates a stronger enterprise capable of generating more stable income and absorbing future growth opportunities.

The pathway is not necessarily linear.

Entrepreneurs may move backward or forward depending on business and household circumstances.

## 14. The Role of Reinvestment

Reinvestment is the central behavioral and economic mechanism of the methodology.

Examples of productive reinvestment include:

- Purchasing additional inventory.
- Purchasing inventory at better prices.
- Expanding product offerings.
- Increasing working capital.
- Acquiring productive equipment.
- Improving business infrastructure.
- Investing in activities that increase future earning capacity.

The objective is to help entrepreneurs distinguish between:

**Business income that can strengthen the enterprise**

and

**Income that must be withdrawn for immediate household needs.**

HRF recognizes that household withdrawals are sometimes unavoidable.

The model therefore seeks to improve both sides of the equation:

**Stronger businesses + stronger household resilience = greater ability to sustain reinvestment.**

## 18. Future Financing Intelligence

Automated credit scoring is **not a Version 1 capability**.

However, the architecture is intentionally being designed so that HRF can eventually develop more sophisticated measures of enterprise performance and financing readiness.

Future capabilities could include:

- Growth Capital Readiness.
- Business performance indicators.
- Cash-flow consistency.
- Repayment behavior.
- Reinvestment patterns.
- Business stability indicators.
- Financing recommendations.

Any future credit or financing intelligence should be developed gradually, validated empirically, and governed responsibly.

The principle is:

**Build the data foundation first; develop financing intelligence only when the evidence and governance are strong enough to support it.**
