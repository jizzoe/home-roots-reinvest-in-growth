# Reinvest-to-Grow™ Methodology Revenue Layers v3.0

**Reinvest-to-Grow™ Revenue Architecture v3.0**

**Building a Diversified Earned-Revenue Model Around Enterprise Growth**

Home Roots Foundation is developing an earned-revenue model in which commercial activities that directly support women entrepreneurs can progressively contribute to the sustainability and expansion of its Haiti operations.

The model is designed around a simple principle:

**HRF earns revenue by creating economic value for entrepreneurs and providing services that strengthen their businesses.**

Revenue generation is therefore not separate from the mission.

It is intended to reinforce the Reinvest-to-Grow™ Methodology.

## 2. Revenue Layer 1 — Enterprise Supply Hub

**Primary Current Revenue Engine**

The Enterprise Supply Hub is the first physical commercial unit of the Reinvest-to-Grow™ model.

HRF purchases or sources commonly needed business inventory and supplies and makes them available to participating entrepreneurs.

Revenue is generated through the **margin between acquisition cost and selling price**.

The Hub therefore serves three purposes simultaneously:

**Entrepreneur Value**

- Lower-cost access to inventory.
- Improved purchasing power.
- Better potential business margins.
- More reliable access to commonly needed products.

**HRF Revenue**

- Transaction margin.
- Potential operating contribution.

**Strategic Validation**

- Demonstrates entrepreneur demand.
- Tests purchasing economics.
- Generates transaction data.
- Provides a physical operating base for the broader model.

The Supply Hub is therefore not the entire HRF business model.

It is the **first earned-revenue engine and a physical validation point for the broader Enterprise Growth Platform.**

## 3. Revenue Layer 2 — Growth Capital

**Financing Revenue**

HRF's growth-capital activities can generate modest financing revenue through appropriately structured business financing.

The purpose of financing is to help entrepreneurs make productive investments such as:

- Inventory expansion.
- Working capital.
- Product expansion.
- Productive business assets.
- Other enterprise-growth investments.

The financing component is designed to complement, rather than replace, the Supply Hub and Enterprise Growth Services.

The strategic logic is:

**Better Purchasing Economics**

- ****

**Appropriate Growth Capital**

- ****

**Business Support**

→

**Greater Capacity for Productive Reinvestment**

→

**Enterprise Growth**

As HRF accumulates reliable longitudinal business information, that information may eventually support more sophisticated financing-readiness assessment.

Automated credit scoring and automated lending decisions are **not assumed to be Version 1 capabilities**.

## 4. Revenue Layer 3 — Mobile Money & Business Transaction Services

Mobile money and related transaction services may provide an additional earned-revenue layer where HRF can create meaningful value for entrepreneurs.

Potential services may include:

- Business-related payment transactions.
- Money transfer services.
- Mobile financial services.
- Other transaction-support services appropriate to the local operating environment.

This layer should be developed selectively and only where the economics, regulatory requirements, and entrepreneur value proposition are sufficiently strong.

It is therefore a **potential operating revenue stream**, rather than a foundational assumption of the model.

## 5. Revenue Layer 4 — Enterprise Business Services

As the Enterprise Growth Platform develops, HRF may offer selected business-support services for which customers or partner organizations are willing to pay.

Potential examples could include:

- Business diagnostics.
- Enterprise training.
- Business-support packages.
- Procurement coordination.
- Specialized enterprise services.
- Logistics or operational support where economically justified.

The principle is important:

**HRF should charge for services when the service creates measurable economic value and the payer is able and willing to pay.**

Not every component of the Reinvest-to-Grow™ Methodology needs to become a fee-based service.

Core entrepreneur support may continue to be subsidized through philanthropic and other mission-aligned capital when necessary.

## 6. Revenue Layer 5 — Platform Participation / Service Revenue

The Enterprise Growth App and broader Enterprise Growth Platform may eventually support a modest participation or service fee.

The concept is different from a conventional SaaS subscription.

The entrepreneur would not simply be paying for bookkeeping software.

Participation could provide access to an integrated set of services such as:

- Business recordkeeping.
- Purchasing coordination.
- Business information.
- Growth-support services.
- Financing-related services.
- Future business intelligence capabilities.

The exact pricing model should be validated through entrepreneur research and pilot experience.

Therefore, HRF should **not yet treat a specific $0.50–$1 monthly fee as a committed financial assumption.**

The strategic opportunity is more important than the exact price:

**As the platform creates increasing economic value for entrepreneurs, a modest participation or service contribution may become an additional source of earned revenue.**

## 7. Revenue Layer 6 — Supplier Partnerships

As the Supply Hub and entrepreneur network grow, HRF may develop commercial relationships with suppliers that benefit from access to aggregated entrepreneur demand.

Potential future models could include:

- Preferred supplier arrangements.
- Volume-based commercial agreements.
- Supplier-supported promotions.
- Distribution partnerships.
- Other value-sharing arrangements.

This should be considered a **future opportunity**, not a core current revenue assumption.

The underlying logic is:

**More Entrepreneurs**

→

**More Aggregated Demand**

→

**Greater Purchasing Volume**

→

**Greater Value to Strategic Suppliers**

→

**Potential Commercial Partnerships**

## 8. Revenue Layer 7 — Platform Licensing / Institutional Access

The longest-term revenue opportunity is potentially extending the Enterprise Growth Platform beyond HRF's own entrepreneur network.

Organizations serving informal and small businesses may face similar operational challenges around:

- Business activity tracking.
- Purchasing.
- Enterprise support.
- Financing administration.
- Business performance measurement.

Once the HRF model and technology are sufficiently proven, HRF could potentially provide platform access to selected:

- Nonprofits.
- Enterprise-development organizations.
- Cooperatives.
- Microfinance organizations.
- Other mission-aligned institutions.

Potential revenue could come from:

- Platform access fees.
- Institutional subscriptions.
- Implementation services.
- Support services.

This is a **long-term strategic option**, not a current revenue forecast.

HRF should first prove the platform within its own operating model.

## 9. What Is Not Revenue

Philanthropic and mission-aligned capital should be presented separately from earned revenue.

This includes:

- Grants.
- Recoverable grants.
- Program-related investments.
- Concessional debt.
- Other catalytic capital.

These sources are essential for financing activities such as:

- Technology development.
- New cluster expansion.
- Product development.
- Innovation.
- Initial growth capital.
- Research and evaluation.

But they should not be represented as earned revenue.

## 11. The Simple HRF Revenue Story

For investors and accelerators, the model can be explained simply:

**HRF's earned-revenue model begins with the Enterprise Supply Hub and growth-financing activities, expands into selected enterprise and transaction services, and creates longer-term opportunities for platform participation, supplier partnerships, and institutional platform access.**

The key is that each potential revenue stream is connected to the same underlying enterprise-growth ecosystem.

## 12. What the Supply Hub Proves

The Supply Hub is particularly important because it provides an early test of whether HRF can create a commercially sustainable enterprise-supporting activity.

The Hub tests:

**Demand**

Will entrepreneurs consistently purchase through the system?

**Economics**

Can HRF achieve viable purchasing and operating margins?

**Entrepreneur Value**

Do lower-cost inputs contribute to stronger business economics?

**Operational Scalability**

Can the model be replicated across clusters?

**Platform Integration**

Can physical purchasing activity increasingly connect with the digital Enterprise Growth Platform?

The Supply Hub is therefore both:

**a revenue engine**

and

**a proof point for the broader model.**

## 14. Strategic Principle

HRF should not position itself as:

**A grant-dependent nonprofit attempting to become a software company.**

Nor should it position itself as:

**A technology company that happens to serve low-income entrepreneurs.**

The stronger positioning is:

**A mission-driven enterprise organization building a diversified economic model around helping women entrepreneurs grow stronger businesses.**

The Enterprise Growth Platform is the infrastructure that increasingly connects those activities.

The Supply Hub generates commercial activity.

Growth Capital supports productive enterprise investment.

Enterprise Services create additional value and potential revenue.

The digital platform improves coordination, business intelligence, and operating leverage.

Over time, the combination can create a more diversified and resilient financial model.

## 15. Investor-Friendly One-Liner

**HRF is building an earned-revenue ecosystem around women's enterprise growth, beginning with Supply Hub margins and growth-financing revenue and expanding over time into enterprise services, platform participation, supplier partnerships, and institutional platform access.**
