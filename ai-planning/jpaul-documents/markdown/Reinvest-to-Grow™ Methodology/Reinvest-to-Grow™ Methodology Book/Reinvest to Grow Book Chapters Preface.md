# Reinvest to Grow Book Chapters Preface

**Reinvest-to-Grow™ Methodology v1.0**

**A Capital Accumulation System for Enterprise Growth**

**Preface**

Economic development practitioners have long sought effective ways to help low-income entrepreneurs build sustainable businesses. For more than four decades, microfinance has been one of the dominant approaches, founded on the belief that expanding access to credit would enable entrepreneurs to invest in their businesses, increase their incomes, and ultimately escape poverty. Millions of entrepreneurs have gained access to financial services that were previously unavailable, yet the evidence suggests that access to credit alone rarely produces broad-based enterprise transformation or sustained local economic development.

At Home Roots Foundation (HRF), our own experience reflected this reality. Over eight years of working alongside women entrepreneurs in Haiti, we observed that many participants increased their incomes after receiving business training, coaching, and financing. Yet relatively few accumulated enough productive capital to fundamentally change the trajectory of their businesses. Many remained vulnerable to the same economic shocks they had faced before entering our programs.

These observations led us to ask a different question.

Rather than asking, **"How can we provide better loans?"**, we began asking:

**"What conditions enable low-income entrepreneurs to consistently accumulate productive capital that compounds into resilient enterprises?"**

This shift fundamentally changed our approach.

We concluded that access to finance, while important, represents only one component of enterprise growth. Entrepreneurs also require lower business costs, reliable market access, disciplined reinvestment, enterprise growth support, and systems that encourage productive capital accumulation over time. When these elements reinforce one another, retained earnings become working capital, working capital becomes productive assets, productive assets strengthen enterprise capability, and stronger enterprises generate greater economic resilience for households and communities.

The Reinvest-to-Grow™ Methodology emerged from this understanding.

Rather than viewing finance as the engine of development, Reinvest-to-Grow™ views **productive capital accumulation** as the central mechanism through which enterprises grow. Financing, collective purchasing, enterprise growth support, performance tracking, and graduated growth opportunities are designed to work together as an integrated system that enables entrepreneurs to retain more of their business earnings and reinvest those earnings into assets that increase future earning capacity.

This document presents Version 1.0 of that methodology.

It is not intended to represent a finished model or definitive solution to enterprise development. Instead, it provides a transparent framework that explains the economic principles underlying the methodology, describes how those principles are translated into practice, and identifies the hypotheses that HRF intends to test through implementation and continuous learning.

We recognize that meaningful enterprise development requires humility. Economic systems are complex, entrepreneurs face diverse constraints, and no single methodology can address every context. Consequently, Reinvest-to-Grow™ should be understood as an evolving methodology—one that will continue to be refined through rigorous measurement, practical experience, independent evaluation, and ongoing dialogue with entrepreneurs, practitioners, researchers, and policymakers.

Ultimately, our aspiration extends beyond improving individual businesses.

We seek to contribute to a broader understanding of how low-income entrepreneurs can accumulate productive capital, build resilient enterprises, create employment, strengthen local markets, and participate more fully in sustainable economic development.

If this methodology succeeds, its greatest contribution will not be the number of loans disbursed, businesses served, or training sessions delivered. Its success will be measured by whether entrepreneurs accumulate the assets, capabilities, and opportunities necessary to build businesses that continue growing long after external support has ended.

**Executive Summary**

**The Central Problem**

For decades, enterprise development efforts have largely focused on expanding entrepreneurs' access to credit and training. While these interventions frequently improve short-term business performance, they often fail to produce sustained enterprise growth. Many entrepreneurs continue operating businesses that remain vulnerable to economic shocks, constrained by limited working capital, high input costs, fragmented supply chains, and insufficient opportunities to accumulate productive assets.

HRF's own experience in Haiti reflects this broader pattern. Although many participating entrepreneurs increased business income, relatively few consistently retained and reinvested enough earnings to transform their enterprises into resilient, growth-oriented businesses.

The challenge, therefore, is not simply increasing income.

The challenge is enabling entrepreneurs to accumulate productive capital that compounds over time.

**Our Hypothesis**

The Reinvest-to-Grow™ Methodology is built upon a single foundational hypothesis:

**Lasting enterprise growth occurs when entrepreneurs consistently retain and reinvest sufficient business earnings to accumulate productive capital that compounds into stronger, more resilient enterprises.**

Income is therefore not the ultimate objective.

Income becomes the mechanism through which productive capital is accumulated.

**The Core Innovation**

Traditional enterprise development programs often address one constraint at a time.

Microfinance expands access to capital.

Training builds business knowledge.

Cash assistance provides temporary liquidity.

Supply chain interventions reduce purchasing costs.

Reinvest-to-Grow™ integrates these complementary functions into a single enterprise growth system designed around one objective: increasing the entrepreneur's capacity to accumulate productive capital.

The methodology currently combines:

- Lower business costs through collective purchasing.
- Graduated growth capital matched to enterprise readiness.
- Enterprise growth support and performance coaching.
- Reinvestment discipline supported by measurable business milestones.
- Performance tracking focused on productive capital accumulation rather than income alone.
- An Enterprise Graduation Pathway that encourages high-potential businesses to evolve into resilient small enterprises capable of creating employment and strengthening local markets.

**Measuring Success**

Unlike many enterprise development programs, Reinvest-to-Grow™ proposes that business success should not be evaluated primarily through changes in income.

Instead, the methodology prioritizes indicators that reflect long-term enterprise capability, including:

- Growth in working capital.
- Productive asset accumulation.
- Inventory expansion.
- Business survival.
- Retained earnings.
- Formal business development.
- Employment generation.
- Participation in local supply chains.

These indicators better capture whether enterprises are becoming more productive, resilient, and capable of sustained growth.

**Long-Term Vision**

The long-term objective of Reinvest-to-Grow™ is not to create dependency on external financing or centralized service delivery.

Rather, it seeks to build local enterprise ecosystems in which entrepreneurs progressively strengthen one another through supply relationships, employment creation, market specialization, and expanding local value chains.

In this vision, successful entrepreneurs become suppliers, employers, distributors, mentors, and market leaders whose growth contributes to broader community prosperity.

The ultimate measure of success is therefore not simply stronger individual businesses, but stronger local economies.

**My Reflections Before We Write Chapter 1**

I'm genuinely pleased with how this begins. However, after writing it, I think we have an opportunity to make the methodology even more distinctive.

The phrase **"Capital Accumulation System for Enterprise Growth"** is strong, but I suspect the document's most original contribution will be something even more fundamental:

**A theory of how productive capital accumulates among low-income entrepreneurs.**

That subtle shift is important. Many organizations describe what they *do*. Very few articulate a coherent explanation of *why enterprise growth happens*. If we can build that theory carefully—grounded in economics, informed by your experience in Haiti, and disciplined by evidence—we'll be creating something much more enduring than a program manual.

As we move into **Chapter 1: Why Traditional Approaches Fall Short**, I want us to begin weaving together four strands:

- **The economic literature** (microfinance, enterprise development, market systems, the "missing middle").
- **HRF's field experience** (what you actually observed over eight years).
- **The conceptual shift** (from lending to productive capital accumulation).
- **The research agenda** (what Reinvest-to-Grow™ is designed to test rather than merely assert).

If we do that well, I believe this document will become much more than an internal framework. It will become the intellectual foundation for HRF's work for the next decade—and a document that can withstand serious scrutiny because it is explicit about both its convictions and its uncertainties. I think that's exactly the standard we should hold ourselves to.

I **do not** want Chapter 1 to become an attack on microfinance.

Many critiques of microfinance become ideological—either "microfinance is the answer to poverty" or "microfinance is a failure." I don't think either position is intellectually honest.

Instead, I want us to write from this position:

**Microfinance solved an important problem—access to finance—but access to finance alone has proven insufficient for sustained enterprise transformation. Reinvest-to-Grow™ builds upon, rather than rejects, the valuable lessons learned from microfinance while addressing several of its structural limitations.**

I think that tone will immediately distinguish HRF from both advocates and critics. It demonstrates that we're trying to advance the field rather than win an argument.
